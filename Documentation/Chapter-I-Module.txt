   CHAPTER I – INTRODUCTION  
    SECTION: A. Background of the Study 
       What is it? 
The background introduces the topic of the project, explains the current situation or problem, 
and presents the proposed solution (your system). It tells the reader why the study matters. 
       Steps to Write: 
1. Start with a general topic (e.g., what is inventory? Why is it important?) 
2. Focus on your institution/program (e.g., ISAT U Dumangas, BIT Automotive) 
3. Describe the current situation or problem (manual process, inefficiencies) 
4. Explain the gap or need for improvement (tool loss, no real-time tracking) 
5. Present your solution (the proposed system) 
6. End with expected benefits (better tracking, CHED compliance, reduced workload) 
   Example: 
Inventory systems are essential tools used in schools, companies, and organizations to 
monitor the availability, condition, and location of equipment and supplies. In educational 
institutions, particularly those offering technical and vocational programs, proper inventory 
management ensures that tools needed for laboratory activities are always available and in 
good condition. 
At Iloilo Science and Technology University Dumangas Campus, the Bachelor of Industrial 
Technology (BIT) major in Automotive Technology includes laboratory courses that require the 
use of various tools and equipment. However, the Automotive Department currently uses a 
manual inventory system. All tool transactions are recorded in notebooks or spreadsheets. This 
manual process is time-consuming, prone to errors, and difficult to monitor in real-time. 
There have been instances where tools were not returned, misplaced, or damaged, but 
no clear record could verify the borrower or transaction details. This affects laboratory 
efficiency and accountability. Moreover, generating reports for tool usage and stock status takes 
too much time. 
Therefore, this study proposes the development of a Tools Inventory System that will 
automate and streamline the inventory process. This system will allow the department to 
record borrowing and returning transactions, monitor stock-in and stock-out activities, manage

The conceptual framework of the study, presented in Figure 1, follows the Input-Process-
Output (IPO) model, which outlines the flow of activities involved in developing the Tools 
Inventory System for the BIT Automotive Program of Iloilo Science and Technology University 
Dumangas Campus. The input stage consists of the essential data and information required by 
the system, including user information, tools and equipment details, stock-in and stock-out 
records, borrowing information, and returning information. These inputs serve as the foundation 
for the system’s operations and ensure that processes are based on complete and accurate data. 
The process stage involves the core activities needed to transform the inputs into the 
desired system output. These include managing user accounts, maintaining the registry of tools 
and equipment, processing stock-in and stock-out transactions, managing borrowing and 
returning transactions, and generating inventory reports. These processes are designed to ensure 
that the system operates efficiently, provides accurate records, and supports the needs of 
laboratory operations. 
The output of the framework is the Tools Inventory System, a functional system that 
improves the management of tools and equipment in the Automotive Department. To ensure the 
system’s quality and effectiveness, the framework includes a system evaluation phase, where the 
developed system is assessed using the ISO 25010 software quality model. This evaluation 
examines the system’s functionality, reliability, usability, and maintainability. The results of the 
evaluation serve as feedback, guiding further improvements by refining inputs and processes to 
better meet the needs of the department.

    SECTION: F. Scope and Limitations 
 
       What is “Scope and Limitations” in a Capstone Project? 
This section is a critical component of Chapter I. It outlines: 
• SCOPE – What your system includes: features, users, environment, modules, 
technologies. 
• LIMITATIONS – What your system does not include: boundaries, excluded features, 
constraints due to time, tools, or design decisions. 
It defines the boundary of your project clearly and sets realistic expectations for the readers, 
clients, and panel members. 
 
      Why is this Section Important? 
✔ It tells readers what to expect from your system and what not to expect. 
✔ It shows that the project is focused, realistic, and feasible. 
✔ It protects you from being criticized for features or goals you never intended to include. 
✔ It reveals a professional level of planning and self-awareness about project limitations. 
 
       HOW TO WRITE THIS SECTION 
   Part 1: Scope (What the system covers) 
Clearly describe: 
• Main system modules (e.g., borrow/return, inventory tracking, report generation) 
• Target users (e.g., admin, lab staff) 
• Deployment environment (e.g., LAN-based, desktop app) 
• Programming tools used 
• Processes that the system automates

Use verbs like: 
• "allows" 
• "includes" 
• "supports" 
• "automates"

   Part 2: Limitations (What the system does NOT cover) 
Clearly describe: 
• Excluded technologies (e.g., no RFID, no mobile access, no cloud)

• "is limited to" 
• "is not designed for"

• "is restricted to"

F. Scope and Limitations 
  Scope: 
This study focuses on the development and implementation of a Tools Inventory System 
designed for the BIT Automotive Program of Iloilo Science and Technology University Dumangas 
Campus. 
The system is developed as a LAN-based application that runs on a desktop environment using 
PHP , MySQL, Apache, and Sublime Text as the development stack. It allows only authorized 
users—specifically the administrator and laboratory staff—to access the system within the 
automotive laboratory. 
The core features of the system include: 
• Managing user accounts for lab staff and admin 
• Maintaining a tool and equipment registry with descriptions and quantity 
• Recording stock-in transactions (incoming tools/equipment) 
• Recording stock-out transactions (tools removed or disposed) 
• Recording borrowing and returning of tools by students (via staff entry) 
• Generating inventory summary reports for print or review 
All system activities are logged by user role and timestamp to maintain accountability. The 
system also allows basic printing of inventory reports and transaction history for 
documentation. 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 
 

=== PAGE 11 ===
 
  Limitations: 
While the developed system successfully automates key inventory functions, it has certain 
limitations due to time, budget, and technical scope: 
1. LAN-Only Access: The system is restricted to use within the laboratory and cannot be 
accessed remotely or via the internet. There is no web-based or cloud deployment 
included in this version. 
2. No Student Login: Students cannot access the system interface. All transactions 
(borrowing/returning) are handled by laboratory staff on their behalf. 
3. No RFID or Barcode Integration: The system does not support barcode scanning or RFID 
tracking of tools. All transactions are manually encoded. 
4. No Notification or Alert System: There are no automatic notifications for overdue 
borrowed tools or low stock levels. 
5. Platform Dependency: The system is tested and optimized for Windows OS only. 
Compatibility with Linux or macOS is not guaranteed. 
6. No Maintenance or Calibration Module: The system does not monitor tool maintenance 
schedules, calibration status, or tool conditions beyond basic remarks. 
7. Limited Reporting Options: Reports generated are basic summaries and do not yet 
include advanced analytics like usage patterns or top-borrowed tools.
